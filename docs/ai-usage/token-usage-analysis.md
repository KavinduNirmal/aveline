# Can other tools' token usage be inferred from the Harness logs?

**Short answer.** It cannot be *correlated*: the hand-written logs carry no token
data, so there is no second measurement to correlate against. It can be
*predicted*, and §8 does that with a Monte Carlo model and a 95 % interval. The
prediction is assumption-bound and its interval is wide: read §8 for the number
and §4 for why it should not be quoted as usage.

This note records the measurement, the arithmetic, and the model. It exists so
the question does not have to be re-argued when the AI usage appendix is written.

---

## 1. What is measured

From `dsh-session-summary.md`, generated by
[`scripts/summarize_dsh_sessions.py`](../../scripts/summarize_dsh_sessions.py)
over `$DSH_HOME` on one developer machine:

| Measure | Aveline workspace | All local workspaces |
|---|---|---|
| Top-level sessions | 71 | 73 |
| Subagent sessions | 210 | 213 |
| Total tokens | ≈4.9 B | ≈4.9 B |
| — cache reads | ≈98.9 % of the total | — |
| — fresh input | ≈36 M | — |
| — output | ≈20 M | — |

The workspace accounts for essentially all of it; the other two session
directories on the machine hold three sessions between them.

The per-request identity `totalTokens = inputTokens + cacheReadTokens +
outputTokens` holds exactly for every request in the store, so these sums
reconcile with a provider-side counter rather than being an artefact of the
parser.

## 2. What is not measured

`grep -ic token` over the three hand-written logs returns hits that are all code
identifiers (`CancellationToken`), design tokens, or product features. **No log
records the token usage of any session.** The same is true of durations: the
"hour" hits are cache TTLs and test names.

What the logs do record is a count of work episodes:

| Log | Entries | Dates | Tool recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kavindu.md` | 51 | 2026-08-25 → 2026-09-21 | mixed: Antigravity, opencode, DeepSeek Harness |
| `Dilud.md` | 82 | 2026-09-07 → 2026-09-18 | Antigravity AI Assistant (82/82) |
| `kaveesha.md` | 13 | 2026-09-07 → 2026-09-19 | Antigravity AI Assistant (13/13) |

## 3. The one correlation that is possible — and what it shows

The only pair of quantities present on both sides is a **date**. Matching
`kavindu.md` entries against Harness sessions by date:

| Date | Log entries | DSH top-level sessions |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-25 → 2026-09-09 (9 dates) | 27 | 0 |
| 2026-09-11 | 6 | 6 |
| 2026-09-13 | 1 | 8 |
| 2026-09-16 | 2 | 4 |
| 2026-09-17 | 0 | 6 |
| 2026-09-18 | 6 | 10 |
| 2026-09-19 | 7 | 13 |
| 2026-09-20 | 1 | 9 |
| 2026-09-21 | 1 | 12 |
| 2026-09-22 | 0 | 3 |

Two things follow, and both argue against extrapolation:

1. **A log entry is not a session.** On dates when the Harness was active,
   kavindu logged 24 entries against 71 top-level Harness sessions — roughly
   **1 entry to 3 sessions**. Only 12 of the 51 entries name the Harness at all.
   A 1:1 mapping is therefore wrong by a factor of about three before any token
   arithmetic begins.
2. **A third of the logged work is invisible to the Harness.** The nine dates
   before 2026-09-11 carry 27 entries with no Harness session at all: that work
   happened in other tools, and no usage figure exists for it anywhere.

## 4. Why the extrapolation does not work

Even granting a mapping, three properties of the Harness figures make a
per-session average unusable:

- **The distribution is heavily skewed.** Per top-level session: median 24.7 M
  total tokens, mean 54.1 M, 90th percentile 155.7 M, maximum 355.3 M. The
  p90 is 6× the median and the maximum is 14×, so the choice between mean and
  median moves the answer more than the mapping does.
- **Cache reads are 98.9 % of the total, and they are a property of the
  harness, not of the work.** A tool with a different context or caching policy
  would report a different total for identical work. "Total tokens" is not
  comparable across tools; output tokens (about 20 M) are the closest thing to a
  comparable volume, and even those depend on how talkative the harness is
  between tool calls.
- **The sessions in this store are unusually heavy.** They include orchestration
  runs with subagent fan-out (210 subagent sessions) and long documentation
  builds. Nothing establishes that an Antigravity session in `Dilud.md` — which
  describes one feature at a time — resembles one of them.

## 5. The arithmetic, for the record

If someone insists on a number, this is what the assumptions produce. **These
are not usage figures and should not be published as such.** Unmeasured entries:
Dilud 82 + Kaveesha 13 = 95.

| Assumption per log entry | At DSH median (24.7 M) | At DSH mean (54.1 M) | At DSH p90 (155.7 M) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 entry = 1 session | 2.35 B | 5.14 B | 14.79 B |
| 1 entry = 3 sessions (the measured ratio) | 7.05 B | 15.43 B | 44.37 B |

The two rows alone differ by 3×, and the columns by 6×. An estimate that can say
"the other two students used somewhere between half and nine times what the first
student did" carries no information, and presenting the midpoint of it as a
measurement would be worse than presenting nothing.

## 6. A prediction, if one is required

A point estimate is unwarranted, but a *predictive distribution* is not: the
Harness store is a large sample of how expensive a work episode is under one
student's working pattern, and that sample can be resampled. The model is
implemented in [`scripts/predict_ai_usage.py`](../../scripts/predict_ai_usage.py)
and is reproducible.

**Model.** Each session is folded into an *episode* — a top-level session plus the
subagent sessions it spawned, however deep — because that is what one logged work
episode corresponds to. An unmeasured entry then draws `k ~ Poisson(3)` episodes
(the measured ratio: 24 of kavindu's entries on dates the Harness was active
against 71 top-level episodes), and each episode draws its cost with replacement
from the observed episode pool (median 32.3 M, mean 69.4 M, maximum 360.9 M).
20,000 trials, seed 20260922.

**Base case** — 95 unmeasured entries (Dilud 82, Kaveesha 13), three episodes per
entry, comparator factor 1.0:

| | low (2.5 %) | median | high (97.5 %) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unmeasured tools | 16.7 B | **20.1 B** | 23.8 B |
| Measured (Harness) | 4.9 B | 4.9 B | 4.9 B |
| **Projected total** | **21.6 B** | **25.0 B** | **28.7 B** |

**The interval is narrow only because the assumptions are held fixed.** The
assumption that matters is the comparator factor — whether one of another
student's episodes is as expensive as one of these. Sweeping both inputs:

| episodes per entry | comparator | projected total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.25 | 7.2 B |
| 1 | 0.5 | 9.4 B |
| 1 | 1.0 | 13.9 B |
| 3 | 0.25 | 9.9 B |
| 3 | 0.5 | 15.0 B |
| 3 | 1.0 | 25.0 B |
| 5 | 1.0 | 37.9 B |

That spread, 7 B to 38 B, is the real uncertainty. `Dilud.md`'s entries are
single-feature work ("Catalog Item Delete Feature", "QR Code Quick View Button &
Modal"); this store's episodes include orchestration runs with subagent fan-out
and long documentation builds, and its mean episode carries 69 M tokens. A
comparator of 1.0 is therefore the least plausible cell in the table, and the
model has no way to put a number on the alternative from the repository alone.

*Figures above were produced on 2026-09-22 and are rounded; the store grows as
sessions stay open, so a re-run will differ in the last digit. The sensitivity
table is the stable part.*

### The cache-hit assumption does not do what it looks like it does

A cache hit **re-splits** a prompt; it does not change how many tokens the tool
sends. The prompt a request transmits is fixed by the tool's context policy, and
`total = fresh input + cache reads + output` is an identity (verified across every
request in the store). Assuming a 95 % hit rate therefore does not reduce the
predicted volume — it moves tokens from the cache-read column into the
fresh-input column, which is the column that carries cost:

| assumed hit rate | fresh input | cache reads |
|---|---|---|
| 90 % | 2.0 B | 18.1 B |
| 95 % | 1.0 B | 19.1 B |
| 99.3 % (observed) | 150 M | 19.9 B |

So the 95 % assumption is a *cost* lever, not a volume lever: at 95 % the same
predicted work is billed roughly 6.7× more input than at the observed 99.3 %.
It also does not explain the 6.8 B versus 4.9 B gap, because the Harness identity
already reconciles within itself.

## 7. What to do instead

1. **Read each tool's own counter.** Every tool involved keeps a usage or billing
   page. DeepSeek's reports roughly 6.8 B tokens for this account (reported by
   the team from the provider console, not measured here). Antigravity's figure
   for Dilud and Kaveesha is the missing number, and it is obtainable from their
   accounts rather than inferred.
2. **Reconcile the Harness gap first.** The provider reports about 6.8 B against
   about 4.9 B recorded across all local Harness workspaces. About 1.9 B is
   unaccounted for — another machine, another client, or a different `DSH_HOME`.
   That is a real discrepancy in a tool that *does* report, and it is worth
   resolving before adding estimated numbers for tools that do not.
3. **Report what is countable.** For the appendix's tool-usage table, sessions
   and tasks per tool are countable and defensible. Token volume belongs in that
   table only for DeepSeek Harness, with the provider console figure named as the
   source. If the prediction in §6 is used at all, it belongs in a footnote that
   names the comparator factor and states that the figure is modelled.

## 8. What is safe to write in the appendix

- DeepSeek Harness: 73 top-level sessions across all local workspaces (71 in
  this one), about 4.9 B tokens, measured from the session store; the provider
  console reports about 6.8 B for the account.
- Antigravity AI Assistant: 95 logged sessions across two students; token volume
  not measured — the tool's console figure is to be recorded here when obtained.
- If a total is required, prefer a sum of measured figures with the unmeasured
  tools named as excluded. A modelled total is defensible only if it is labelled
  as modelled, with the comparator factor stated and the sensitivity range shown.
